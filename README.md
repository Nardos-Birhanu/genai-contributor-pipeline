# Generative AI and the Newcomer-to-Contributor Pipeline in Online Knowledge Communities

## Research Overview

This repository contains the development workflow for a miniature computational social science research project investigating how generative AI may influence newcomer progression in online knowledge communities.

The project focuses on whether  Gen-AI answers change the traditional pathway through which newcomers become contributors.

The study uses Stack Overflow as an empirical setting and explores the relationship between generative AI, newcomer participation, and the sustainability of knowledge production communities.



## Research Motivation

Online knowledge communities depend not only on existing contributors but also on their ability to continuously transform newcomers into future contributors.

Previous research has examined participation patterns, contributor retention, and the effects of generative AI on online activity. However, an open question remains regarding whether generative AI affects the transition process through which newcomers develop into active contributors.

This project investigates this potential disruption of the newcomer-to-contributor pipeline.



## Working Research Question

How has the emergence of generative AI affected the transition of newcomers into contributors in online knowledge communities?

The research question is currently being refined through literature validation, data feasibility assessment, and methodological design.



## Research Direction

The project follows a computational social science research workflow:

1. Validate the research gap through systematic literature review.
2. Define theoretical foundations and concepts.
3. Operationalize theoretical concepts into measurable variables.
4. Evaluate data feasibility.
5. Design and implement the computational analysis pipeline.
6. Analyze newcomer contribution pathway.



## Planned Methodological Approach

The planned analysis combines:

- longitudinal cohort analysis,
- newcomer-to-contributor conversion measurement,
- survival analysis for contribution timing,
- potential causal inference approaches such as Difference-in-Differences if assumptions and data feasibility support their use.
